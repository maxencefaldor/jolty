# Changelog

Every version of jolty and what it changes.
The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## Unreleased

The first release, 0.1.0.

### Added

- `NOTICE`, the copyright notices and terms of the software jolty's device models derive from: BSIM3v3.3.0 of the UC Berkeley BSIM Research Group, which jolty's BSIM3 is derived from, and ngspice, whose implementations its diode, JFET, bipolar transistors, MOSFETs of levels 1 and 3 and VDMOS follow; each model's documentation carries its source's notice, and the wheels and the `jolty-models` crate carry the file.
- A transfer function analysis, `TransferFunction(source, output, reference=None)`, SPICE's `.tf`: the gain at DC from an independent source to a net's voltage or a voltage source's current, the resistance the source sees and the one the output sees, infinite where no current can flow, in reports, results files (version 7 of their format) and the MCP server's guide.
- A wheel for free-threaded Python 3.15 beside the regular one, its extension declaring it needs no GIL, so that a script's own threads, a batch's `each` and its custom measurements among them, run at once; CI runs the tests on it, and a test checks that importing the engine leaves the GIL off and that simulations from eight Python threads at once find the bits one finds.
- Circuits as Python build scripts of reusable components, compiled into a canonical, validated circuit format, with every problem named by instance path and script line.
- A native engine in Rust: modified nodal analysis with charge-oriented device models, sparse LU, Newton iteration with SPICE's convergence aids and pseudo-transient continuation past the folds where they stall, an operating point solved once for every analysis about it and the checks at it, analyses that fail rather than fill the memory, a transient's results and the dense matrices of pole-zero analyses and periodic steady states held to one `memory` option, and the operating point, DC sweep, AC, transient, periodic steady state, sensitivity, stability, noise and pole-zero analyses, and a search for every DC solution by deflation; a transient steps onto each jump of a behavioral source and restarts its integration there, starts each step's Newton iteration on the line through its last two points, and judges every step's error, the first after a corner by the curvature it shares with the second, its estimate filtered through the step's own equations so that a component settling within the step is not read as an error, and held to a `truncation_tolerance` that means what SPICE's `TRTOL` does.
- A sparse LU solver for circuits' matrices, after KLU: a block triangular form that leaves a supply's many entries unfactored, a minimum degree ordering of each block, and threshold pivoting that keeps to the ordering's diagonal; each Newton iteration refactors with the last pivots, searching afresh only from one that has become too small, and keeps the factors of a Jacobian that has not changed, so a linear circuit's transient factors only when its step or its formula changes; it eliminates the unknowns whose pivots are proven, a MOSFET's Meyer displacements, before it factors, and recovers them after each solve, so that a chain of 500 inverters factors 504 of its 3,504 unknowns, as many as ngspice's matrix holds; and a singular circuit's error names the unknowns its equations leave undetermined.
- Device models: resistors, capacitors and inductors with voltage coefficients, resistors' flicker noise, coupled inductors, sources and their waveforms, controlled sources, behavioral sources, voltage- and current-controlled switches, the diode, the JFET, the Gummel-Poon transistor with its excess phase, level 1 and level 3 MOSFETs, BSIM3, a photocell, and logic devices simulated by an event scheduler beside the analog circuit.
- The power MOSFET of ngspice and LTspice, `vdmos(...)`, ngspice 47's VDMOS: its square law softened below the threshold, mobility degradation, triode multiplier and quasi-saturation, its gate-drain capacitance from `CGDMIN` to `CGDMAX` held as a conserved charge, a body diode whose breakdown is the transistor's, its gate, drain, source and body resistances, ngspice's temperature scaling of each and its noise, n- and p-channel, with every derivative exact; it heats like the other semiconductors, and the SPICE reader reads vendors' `VDMOS` cards, `pchan` and LTspice's annotations among them, and `M` elements of three to five nodes, which the exporter writes back for ngspice.
- The short-channel MOSFET of most CMOS processes from 0.8 µm to 0.13 µm, `bsim3(...)`, ngspice 47's BSIM3v3.3, SPICE's level 8 and 49: its threshold of short and narrow channels and drain-induced barrier lowering, poly depletion, `mobmod` 1 to 3, velocity saturation, output resistance, `rdsw`, substrate current, the charges of `capmod` 0 to 3 with `xpart`, its junctions, every parameter's length, width and area dependence, ngspice's temperature scaling and parameter checks, and `noimod` 1 to 6, with every derivative exact, its card's parameters by the manual's names in SI units; it heats like the other semiconductors, and the SPICE reader reads level 8 and 49 cards of version 3.3 and selects a binned card's bin by `LMIN` to `WMAX` as ngspice does, which the exporter writes back for ngspice.
- The bipolar transistor of integrated circuits, `vbic(...)`, ngspice 47's VBIC 1.2, SPICE's level 4 and 9: beside the Gummel-Poon transistor's effects, its parasitic substrate transistor, weak avalanche, quasi-saturation, base-emitter breakdown, reach-through and smoothed junction capacitances, its excess phase as a network of its own, ngspice's temperature scaling, and its noise, with every derivative exact; it heats like the other semiconductors, and the SPICE reader reads its cards and Q elements of three to five nodes, which the exporter writes back for ngspice.
- Memristors: VTEAM's, voltage-controlled with thresholds, and HP's linear ion drift, current-controlled, each with a linear or exponential law and a window at either bound; their state holds at an operating point and moves in a transient, and they are written for ngspice as behavioral currents.
- Devices' dimensionless states, a memristor's and a photocell's response, as unknowns of their own quantity, which results read with `state(name)` and plots draw on a panel of their own.
- Vendors' SPICE macromodels read as components, and any circuit written as a SPICE netlist for ngspice, behavioral expressions written from their trees so ngspice computes what jolty does.
- Independent sources read from SPICE with their `PULSE`, `SIN` and `PWL` waveforms over time, as jolty's exporter writes them, where the reader read their DC level alone.
- Circuits written in Xyce 7.10.0's dialect, `export.netlist(..., dialect=export.XYCE)`, and run in Xyce as a separate program by `jolty.spice.xyce.run`, which reads the values it prints and the times it reports: resistors, capacitors, inductors and coupled inductors, independent and controlled sources, the diode, the JFET, the Gummel-Poon transistor and MOSFETs of levels 1 and 3, at the temperature where Xyce's thermal voltage is jolty's; a device or a parameter Xyce lacks or defines otherwise, BSIM3 3.3.0, VDMOS, VBIC, excess phase or expressions among them, is refused by name; `tests/test_xyce.py` checks operating points, DC sweeps, AC responses, noise and transients against Xyce where it is installed, naming each of Xyce's departures it leaves out.
- One way to run each other simulator, alike for both: `jolty.spice.ngspice.execute` and `jolty.spice.xyce.execute` each write a deck's lines into a directory of their own, run the program with a timeout, and return an `Executed` of what it printed, its exit `code`, its `seconds` from its start to its exit, writing the deck left out, and the `values` file the deck wrote, `VALUES`; ngspice's takes its `compatibility` mode, Xyce's its `launch` command, such as its parallel build's, and a run past its timeout raises `NgspiceTimeoutError` or `XyceTimeoutError`; `jolty.spice.xyce.Result.analysis_seconds` is the time Xyce reports for its analyses.
- SKY130's transistors, foundry devices the engine checks but does not solve: their circuits' operating points, DC sweeps, small-signal responses, transients and noise run in ngspice with the pinned process design kit's models, fetched and checked once, at the plan's and its corners' process corners, and Monte Carlo samples draw the kit's mismatch.
- Ratings a part's maker states on its voltages, currents, power and temperature, on a device or a component's ports, each bounded above, below or both, held to every operating point and transient.
- Thermal networks of thermal resistances, heat capacities and heat sources, whose nets are temperatures above the ambient, solved with the circuit.
- Self-heating: resistors, diodes, JFETs, bipolar transistors, VBIC transistors, MOSFETs, BSIM3 MOSFETs and VDMOS that run at their thermal net's temperature and heat it by the power they dissipate, with exact derivatives in every analysis.
- Devices at temperatures of their own, held or offset from the simulation's, read from SPICE's `TEMP` and `DTEMP` and PSpice's `T_ABS` and `T_REL_GLOBAL`, and written for ngspice.
- Real parts, each a model with its maker's number and ratings, heated on request: the 2N3904, 2N3906 and 1N4148, and four power MOSFETs fitted to their datasheets.
- Rules on a circuit's structure, on its values against the E series, at its operating point (a net only gmin holds, another DC solution, a mode that grows), on its loops' margins and on its ratings; schematics drawn from the connections alone, plots of every analysis, and a 3D viewer that plays any simulation on the layout.
- Specs measured on every result, at every temperature and corner, over Monte Carlo samples of tolerances, and at the extremes a worst-case analysis sets them to: on any number of a device's parameters, by path, in linear or logarithmic spreads, and shared by lots.
  A yield states its 95 % Wilson interval, exact at both ends, which `jolty.specs.wilson(passing, samples)` gives of any proportion.
- Fidelity: components characterized into logic cells, run in their place, and spot-checked against their circuits.
- Exact sensitivities of every unknown to every parameter, at the operating point, along DC sweeps, over frequency and over time, each parameter's derivatives those of its device's instance alone, in a block of its own, so that at the operating point a parameter costs what its device does rather than what the circuit does.
- Pole-zero analysis by the QZ algorithm, jolty's own after LAPACK's, which gives the same numbers on every machine, on the linearized circuit's pencils, with specs on the least damping and the dominant pole, agreeing with ngspice.
- The periodic steady state of a driven circuit, found by shooting with the monodromy matrix carried along each period, which keeps its start's place along neutral directions, such as a memristor's state its drive leaves alone, as a transient would.
- Noise analysis by the adjoint method: every device's thermal, shot and flicker noise at an output, each source's share, the noise referred to an input, and specs on noise densities and RMS noise, all agreeing with ngspice 43 and later, whose MOSFETs, as jolty's, give each parallel copy the flicker noise of its own gate.
- A parts catalog that prices a circuit's bill of materials: by value and tolerance, or by part number for numbered devices and components, in packages, never with an obsolete part.
- KiCad netlists of a component, for laying out its board: each part of its bill of materials a footprint from its catalog part, its pads on the nets its part's pins name, with every problem that keeps an instance off the board named.
- Results that record what a plan's or an analysis's `record` names beside the unknowns, by path or pattern, with exclusions after `!` as in a `.gitignore`, such as `Plan(..., record=["X1/**", "!X1/bias/**"])` or `simulate --record M1`: a device's terminals' currents and charges, small-signal model and variables, and a component instance's ports, summed in the engine; operating points and transients also record the instances rated for a current or a power, which the checks read, so a transient holds about its unknowns alone, and reading what a result did not record says how to record it.
  What an analysis records is read of the devices at each solution and changes none of its numbers: an analysis that records other than the one before it takes the operating point that one solved, as any other analysis about it does, where it used to solve it again.
- A command line, an MCP server and a Python package, every command defined once; on Python 3.15 a command loads only the modules it uses, so one that draws nothing starts without matplotlib.
- The setting `analysis_log`, `JOLTY_ANALYSIS_LOG`: a file that each analysis a simulation runs, alone or in a batch, appends a line to as it starts, whatever runs it, the command line, the MCP server or a script, its kind, the runs it runs on and the time, as `jolty.simulate.Logged` reads it, so a session's simulations can be counted.
- `jolty.cli.CommandLine` and `jolty.cli.run`, the base of jolty's own command line in its conventions, for a script's command line beside it: kebab-case options, flags without values, lists without JSON, never the environment, and an invalid value named with what it accepts.
- A reference of every model's parameters, each with the default the engine takes and its description, generated from the engine's own parameter types.
- Benchmarks: blind tasks for one agent, from Koza's patented circuits, AnalogCoder's problems and a cost-down, each judged by a scorer the agent never sees.
- A comparison of what jolty gives an agent, in `benchmarks/compare.py`: sessions of one agent on each task with jolty and with ngspice alone, each arm with its own prompt, files and tools and a counted wrapper around its simulator, the ngspice arm's netlists read by jolty's SPICE reader and judged by the same scorers, and a report of each arm's success rate with its Wilson interval, its turns, tool calls and simulations, and the arms' difference on each task; a stub agent proves it without a paid session, simulating through each of jolty's front ends.
  A session of the jolty arm counts every analysis jolty ran, whatever ran it, its command line, its MCP server or a script through its Python package, from the log jolty keeps when its setting `analysis_log` names one, where a wrapper would see only the command line's runs; one of the ngspice arm counts the runs of ngspice, each of which may run several analyses.
  Each session runs in a configuration of its own, with no settings, `CLAUDE.md`, memory or MCP servers of the user's, and in the platform's sandbox, Seatbelt on macOS and bubblewrap on Linux, which reads nothing of the user's files and writes only the session's own, and so does the scoring of its work, which runs the agent's scripts; Claude Code runs only in one, once it is found there to start and to hold credentials, for free.
  The report gives the failure kinds: how each session ended, finished, out of time, turns or budget, or in an error, each limit optional, and of what kinds its work fell short, from the scorer's own verdict, with the agent's last report beside it.
- Benchmarks of speed: fixtures from a bipolar amplifier to a mesh of 90,000 nodes, in `benchmarks/speed/`, timed by criterion in Rust and end to end in Python by `scripts/bench`, which writes the times by commit and flags each change over 5% from a baseline's, with ngspice's and Xyce's times beside them for context.
- One command line for every suite of `benchmarks/`, `scripts/bench`, its precision, robustness and coverage suites modes of it; one runner per simulator, `benchmarks/simulators.py`, for jolty, ngspice with Sparse 1.3 and with KLU, and Xyce serially and on MPI ranks, which every suite runs them by; and one module of what every suite records, `benchmarks/measuring.py`: the load, the commit with its `-dirty` mark, the machine, and the results files, each `<suite>/results/<commit>.json`.
- A corpus of hard circuits, `scripts/bench --robustness`: Schmitt triggers, latches, an SRAM cell, a ring started from rest, an impasse point and others of jolty's own, and CircuitSim90's decks of level 3 MOSFETs and bipolar transistors, fetched at a pinned commit by the manifest `benchmarks/robustness/external.json` and never copied; each fixture runs in jolty and in ngspice, each in a process its timeout stops, and each outcome is classified alike, against every DC solution jolty finds, as agreed, disagreed, solved, failed, timed out or unsupported, and each simulator's failure rate is stated, DC and transient apart, with its Wilson interval.
  `jolty.spice.ngspice.read` reads the plots of any ASCII raw file ngspice writes.
- Benchmarks of precision, in `benchmarks/precision/`, run by `scripts/bench --precision`: 16 fixtures with exact answers, closed forms and linear circuits solved in higher precision by mpmath, among them ladders and random networks of up to 1,000 nodes whose time constants spread over six decades; each run in jolty and in ngspice 47, with Sparse 1.3 and with KLU, at its defaults and along sweeps of the relative tolerance alone and of every tolerance together, scored by the correct digits of its worst point against the exact answer there, beside its engine time, steps, rejections and Newton iterations, and drawn as a work-precision diagram per fixture.
- A coverage matrix, `scripts/bench --coverage`, in `benchmarks/coverage/`: 65 rows of analyses, device models, SPICE's syntax and outputs, each a fixture, the smallest circuit with a known answer, run in jolty, ngspice 47 with Sparse 1.3 and with KLU, and Xyce 7.10, each cell judged against the answer as agreed, disagreed with its numbers, failed, or unsupported with the simulator's message; LTspice's, Spectre's, HSPICE's and AFS's cells cite their documents; it lists each simulator's rows that another has.
- A transient's statistics count its work, its Newton iterations and its sparse solver's factorizations, refactorizations, fallbacks and reuses, and with the `profile` option measure where its time goes: preparing, evaluating, assembling, factoring, solving, step control and recording.
- Measurements the engine reads in Rust: every measurement of `jolty.specs` resolves its signals and units against a result, and the engine computes its number from the result's arrays, so that a batch of runs can measure its runs without Python; the arithmetic follows numpy's operation for operation and reads the bits numpy read, but for a distortion, whose amplitudes are the discrete Fourier transform's sums rather than numpy's fast transform's, and a requirement whose arithmetic leaves the domain of Python's `math`, such as the RMS over a sweep that turns back, is unmeasured rather than an error.
- A circuit elaborated once, the engine's `Elaborated`, which every run of it shares across threads: its unknowns, its matrix's pattern and the sparse solver's ordering of it, and a table of every number its devices write, by path then by name.
  A run is a `Simulator` of it at `Values`, the numbers it replaces and the temperature, whose devices' instances are prepared for those values in their groups; `Simulator::reset` prepares again those whose numbers or temperature other values change and forgets everything the runs before left, so a reset run equals one made afresh, bit for bit and in its work.
  Values that give a device another structure, as a resistance crossing 1 Ω carries its current as an unknown, run on the circuit restructured for them, which `Elaborated::structure_of` names by a key so that each structure is elaborated once.
- Device models as kernels: every model, from the resistor to the logic devices' connect elements, is a pure function of one instance's prepared numbers, its memory and its local unknowns, which allocates nothing and never panics, and a device that heats is its kernel's instance at its thermal net's temperature.
  The engine evaluates the instances of one kernel and one shape together, each into its own block of a slab, on one thread or, once the options' `parallelism` sets the instances a task takes and the circuit has more, across the pool's, every group at once, and adds the blocks to the circuit's equations in one fixed order, the same bits however many threads evaluate them; a run prepares again only the instances whose numbers or temperature change, a sweep's or a warm chain's included.
  The observer reads each recorded device's terminals and small-signal model from its instance's block of the slab, as the evaluation that solved the circuit wrote it, rather than from a copy of the device evaluated again.
  A MOSFET of level 1 or 3 differentiates each junction's current and charge and each overlap's charge with respect to the voltage across it alone, sums the conductances and capacitances among its gate, bulk, drain and source as it computes them, and writes each once: the 500-inverter chain's transistors evaluate in 200 ns rather than 226, and one without an oxide in 131 rather than 157.
  Results move by rounding, by at most 3.6e-14 V on that chain and 2.6 µV, at an edge, on the benchmarks' buck converter.
  A value of more than 1,024 contributions, such as a supply's, joined to every device, is gathered from copies its instances write beside one another as they evaluate, in the order they add, rather than from a place of nearly every block; on the chain of 50,000 inverters a parallel evaluation's gather on eight threads takes 2.9 ms, where it took 3.5 to 3.7, the same bits.
  A node's contributions now add in that order, so results move by rounding: the order of the devices and that of the groups differ by at most `(n − 1)·ε` of the magnitudes of the `n` terms summed, and by `ε` at most on the differential corpus and the examples.
  A linear device, a resistor or a capacitor without voltage coefficients, an inductor, coupled inductors, a controlled source, a thermal resistance or a thermal capacitance, is evaluated once a run, for its conductances and capacitances, which sum once a run into the matrix every Newton iteration starts from; each iteration writes its currents and charges from them, leaving out the terms that are zero in every such device of its kind, and evaluates only the devices that are not linear.
  On the benchmarks' 100 × 100 RC mesh the evaluation takes 125 ms of the transient's 1.14 s, where it took 197 ms of 1.21 s; results move by rounding, by at most 3.7e-15 V there and 7.3e-11 V on the amplifier, not at all on the buck converter and the inverters' chain, and stay the same bits on any number of threads.
- Monte Carlo samples and worst-case runs share one circuit elaborated per structure, `jolty.simulate.Elaborated`, and make only their devices again: they find the same numbers, bit for bit, without elaborating the circuit from its JSON text.
- A batch executor in the engine, `jolty_engine::batch`, which runs many runs of one circuit in one call on every thread of the caller's rayon pool: its variants list values, step a grid of parameters and the temperature whose warm lines run as chains, each run starting from the one before as a DC sweep's levels do, draw Monte Carlo samples from their index, or run every variant of one at every variant of another; each run runs the batch's analyses and reads its measures, a run that fails is an outcome that says why and the batch goes on, and a structure the runs meet is elaborated once.
  Every outcome and the summary are the same bits at any number of threads and for any split of the runs into ranges.
- Small-signal responses, noise, loop gains and sensitivities spread their frequencies and right-hand sides across threads once the options' `parallelism` sets how few a task takes, which it does not by default, and find the same bits as on one thread: each run of frequencies starts from the pivots the run before it leaves, and is taken again where a pivot changed.
- A large circuit's matrix factors in parallel once the options' `parallelism` sets `max_unknowns_per_interior`, which it does not by default: each block of its block triangular form with more unknowns is ordered by George and Liu's nested dissection into interiors of at most so many and the separators between them, the interiors factor at once by the left-looking elimination, each pivoting within its own rows, and each separator assembles the updates of the parts it separates, in their order, into a dense front it factors once they are done; a column whose pivot its node cannot take is delayed to the separator above rather than perturbed, a refactorization keeps every node's pivots while they hold, and the solves run up and down the same tree.
  The factors and solutions are the same bits on any number of threads, and agree with the serial factorization's within its rounding; on the benchmarks' mesh of 90,000 nodes a refactorization takes 108 ms on one thread and 28 on eight, where the serial one takes 289.
  Each subtree of the dissection, down to its interiors, is a task whichever thread frees takes, so a slow core holds up no other's share, and the rows above a block, such as those a supply joins, take in its solution in runs of 1,024 at once; on the chain of 50,000 inverters a solve on eight threads takes 1.4 ms, where it took 1.85 to 2.7.
  A row above a block with more than 1,024 entries in its columns, such as a supply's equation joined to every device, takes them in in chunks of 1,024, the chunks' totals then in their order, as `jolty_numerics::reduce::fold` adds, so that they spread too, on one thread or many alike; the results of a circuit with such a row move by rounding, and those of every other stay as they were.
- A large circuit's Newton iteration spreads its work on each unknown and each stored entry across threads once the options' `parallelism` sets `min_unknowns_inside` and the circuit has that many unknowns: combining the devices' contributions into the matrix and the residual, the tolerance checks and the step's update each run in chunks of 1,024 values that the threads take as they free, so a fast core takes more of them than a slow one, and the sums a deflated search takes over the unknowns add in chunks of 1,024 terms in one fixed order, `jolty_numerics::reduce`, the same bits on any number of threads.
  A transient's step control spreads the same way: each step's history terms, prediction and charges' derivatives, the divided differences that judge it, their filtering through the step's own equations, the largest ratio of an error to its tolerance and the shortest step allowed, found chunk by chunk and then over the chunks in their order, and the test of the trapezoidal formula's ringing; on the benchmarks' chain of 50,000 inverters, step control took 1.33 s of a short transient on 12 threads, and takes 0.37.
- A run that spreads its work uses at most its parallelism's `threads`: one whose `threads` are two or more, fewer than the pool it is called in has, runs on a pool of exactly that many threads of its own, made for each analysis, which every task it spreads runs on, where its devices' tasks and the gathers of their contributions ran on every thread of its caller's pool.
- A DC sweep of any number of a device's parameters, named by its `parameter`, beside the level of an independent source; a sweep prepares its device's instance again at each level, so no device holds a level of its own.
- The engine's threads, `threads` in the settings or `JOLTY_THREADS`, one per processor by default, and `jolty.simulate.threads` for a block of code: an analysis that spreads its work runs on a pool of the engine's own, never rayon's global one, and every call that runs the engine, elaborates a circuit or checks one releases the interpreter.
- Batches from Python: `Simulation.batch(analyses, variants)` runs many runs of one circuit in one call to the engine, across its threads, each run finding what a simulation at its values alone finds, bit for bit, at any number of threads, and returns a `BatchResult` of NumPy columns, one row per run, which a sink in the engine writes at each run's row as it ends: the values each run took, whether each analysis ended and why it failed, what each measurement read and why one read nothing, and the unknowns it traces at each point of an operating point, a DC sweep, a response and a transient's `times`.
  The engine reads every measurement of `jolty.specs` itself; one a script writes, and a function given as `each`, read each run's results under the interpreter's lock as it ends.
  A circuit of a foundry's devices runs each run in ngspice, as many at once as the engine has threads.
- Variants in `jolty.plan`: `Explicit` runs at listed `Variant`s of numbers and a temperature, a `Grid` of `Axis` values of parameters and the temperature, its lines warm chains if asked, and the `Product` of two, beside `MonteCarlo`; a plan's `variants` run its analyses at its own conditions and at every corner, and the report states the runs, how many failed, each requirement's spread and the yield, with its Wilson interval.
- A batch plots the distribution of its runs, not each run: `jolty simulate` draws a histogram of each requirement a plan's Monte Carlo samples or variants read, against its bounds, and the envelope of each plotted net along each analysis, its least, median and greatest value, and saves the batches as `<script>.batches.npz`.
- A DC sweep of a parameter from Python, `DcSweep("R1", levels, parameter="resistance")`, whose result names the parameter and has no quantity; results files are in version 6 of their format, which names it.
- Shards: `jolty simulate SCRIPT --samples START:STOP` runs those samples of a plan's Monte Carlo analysis alone, at the plan's own conditions and at every corner, as a command with no state of its own that any machine runs, and writes them whole or not at all to `<script>.shards/shard-START-STOP.npz`, with a fingerprint of the circuits, the plan, the options, jolty's version and build and the platform, which leaves out the script lines that placed the circuits' instances, so that a script run from any directory, on any machine, makes shards that merge; `jolty merge DIRECTORY` and the MCP tool `merge_shards` refuse shards whose fingerprints differ or whose samples leave a gap or overlap, naming them, join their batches in the order of their samples, and report the analysis, save its batch and plot it as one run of every sample does, bit for bit.
  A shard's outcome is its measurements, and the probes named alone besides, and its report warns when the shards of every sample would hold more than 10 GB.
  `examples/cluster/launch.py` runs a plan's shards over a pool of local processes, again where one failed, and merges them.
- Batches of a range of runs: `Simulation.batch(..., runs=range(start, stop))`, whose rows are those of the whole batch's that the range names, and `BatchResult.joined`, which joins the results of ranges into the whole's; a result's reasons are named once each, in the order its rows first name them, so a range's and a join's are those of a batch of their runs.
  Results, batch and shard files are written in bytes that depend on what they hold alone.
- The engine's parallelism from Python: `Options(parallelism=Parallelism(min_solves_per_task=16))`, and `jolty simulate --min-solves-per-task 16` in place of the plan's, spread a run's frequencies and right-hand sides, its devices' evaluation, its Newton iteration's work on each unknown or its matrix's factorization across the engine's threads once each threshold is set, which none is by default; every setting finds the same bits on any number of threads, and every one but `max_unknowns_per_interior`, which orders the factorization's operations, the same bits as none.
- Each transistor's region of operation and its margins to the regions' boundaries, as its model's equations distinguish them: off, weak inversion, linear or saturated for the MOSFETs, BSIM3, VDMOS and the JFET, with `vgs − vth` and `vds − vdsat`, and cut off, active, saturated or reversed for the Gummel-Poon and VBIC transistors, with their junctions' voltages; each kernel reports them among its variables, an operating point that records a transistor reads its region by path, `point.region("M1")`, a simulation's `transistors` names them all, and `jolty simulate`'s report and its JSON list them at the plan's own operating points, those nearest a boundary first; results files keep each device's regions and margins, in version 10 of their format.

### Changed

- Each concept has one name in every layer, the engine, the Python API, the extension, the files, the command line, the MCP server and the docs, after the naming glossary the owner decided on 15 November; there are no aliases for the old names.
  The analyses' results, reports and views are one family: analysis `X`, result `XResult`, report `XReport`, kind `x`, `read_x`: `jolty.results.OperatingPointResult`, `OperatingPointsResult`, `DcSweepResult`, `AcResult`, `TransientResult`, `SensitivityResult`, `StabilityResult`, `NoiseResult`, `PoleZeroResult`, `TransferFunctionResult` and their union `AnalysisResult`, in Python and in the engine, for `OperatingPoint`, `OperatingPoints`, `Sweep`, `Response`, `Waveforms`, `Sensitivities`, `LoopGain`, `NoiseSpectrum`, `PolesZeros`, `Transfer` and `Result` (`jolty_engine::Outcome`); the reports `OperatingPointReport`, `OperatingPointsReport`, `DcSweepReport`, `AcReport` and `TransferFunctionReport` for `PointReport`, `PointsReport`, `SweepReport`, `ResponseReport` and `TransferReport`; the measures' view `jolty_engine::measure::AnalysisResult` for `Results`, its variants after their analyses, and `Measure.read_operating_point`, `read_operating_points`, `read_dc_sweep`, `read_ac`, `read_stability` and `read_pole_zero` for `read_point`, `read_solutions`, `read_sweep`, `read_response`, `read_loop` and `read_poles`; and the extension's dictionaries after what they carry, `_core.AcResult` for `ResponseOutcome` and the like, `_core.Work`, `Profile`, `Statistics`, `NoiseInput`, `Problem`, `Diagnostic`, `Excursion` and `BatchResult` for their `XOutcome`s.
  A number read from a result is a measure in every layer, as the engine and the extension named it: `jolty.specs.Measure`, `MeasureError`, `Requirement.measure`, `Simulation.batch(measures=...)` and `BatchResult.measures`, for `Measurement`, `MeasurementError`, `measurement` and `measurements`, in batch and shard files too, in version 2 of each format.
  What an unknown is, a voltage, a current, a temperature or a state, is its nature, Verilog-AMS's term: `jolty.unknowns.Nature`, `Unknown.nature` and every result's and report's `nature`, `NoiseReport.referred_nature` and `jolty_models::Nature`, for `Quantity`, `quantity` and `referred_quantity`, so that `Quantity` is only a number with its dimension; results files say `nature`, in version 11 of their format.
  The file, line and function of the script that placed an instance or stated a requirement is its source location, as C++'s `std::source_location`: `jolty.ir.SourceLocation`, `jolty_ir::SourceLocation` and the fields `source_location` of instances, locations, requirements and verdicts, for `Source` and `source`, so that `source` is only an analysis's independent source; circuit files say `source_location`, in version 2 of their format, and the guide's "Provenance" of instances is "Source locations".
  Where an instance is, its path and its source location, is a location in every layer, as `jolty_engine::elaborate::Location`: `jolty.checks.Location` and `_core.Location` for `checks.Instance` and `InstanceOutcome`, so that `Instance` is only the circuit's.
  A field that names an instance by its path is `instance` in every layer: `jolty.results.Excursion.instance`, `jolty_engine::sensitivity::Parameter::instance` and `jolty_engine::noise::NoiseContribution::instance`, for `path` and `device`, so results files say `instance`, in version 12 of their format.
  The values one unknown takes along an analysis are its trace in every layer: `Simulation.batch(traces=...)`, `BatchResult.traces`, `jolty.results.Traces`, `--traces`, `SimulateRequest.traces`, `PlotRequest.traces`, `plots.plot(..., traces)` and `plots.default_traces`, for `traced`, `Traced`, `--probes`, `probes` and `default_probes`, in batch files too, in version 3 of their format; a logic net's levels over time are a `jolty.results.LogicTrace`, for `Trace`, so that a probe is only a stability analysis's.
  What a device keeps of its own beyond its terminals' currents and charges is its variables in every layer, as the engine, the extension and `RecordedDevice.variables` named them: the results' `variables`, for `readings`, in results files too, in version 13 of their format, so that a reading is only a measure's value.
  An instance's name inside its component is its `name`, as every other named thing has it: `ir.Instance.name`, `jolty_ir::Instance::name`, `Builder.place(item, name=...)`, `Placed.name` and the layout's placed instances' `name`, for `id`; circuit files say `name`, in version 3 of their format, and so do the 3D viewer's scenes.
  A device instance's model is its `model` in every layer, as `Device.model` holds the same name: `ir.DeviceOf.model`, for `device`; circuit files say `"of": {"model": ...}`, in version 4 of their format, and so do the 3D viewer's scenes.
  A logic net's value is its logic level in every layer, so that a level is only a rule's: `jolty.devices.LogicLevel`, defined once, and `jolty.devices.format_logic_level`, which writes one as text; `OperatingPointResult.logic_level(net)`, `DcSweepResult.logic_level(net)` and `TransientResult.logic_level(net)` for `level(net)`; `jolty.report.NetLevel` for `Level`; and `jolty_models::LogicLevel` for `Level`.
  Bounds have the shape `min_x` and `max_x`: the options `max_dc_iterations`, `max_transient_iterations` and `max_memory`, in plans and in the engine, for `dc_iterations`, `transient_iterations` and `memory`; and `OperatingPoints(max_solutions=...)`, for `most`.
  A confidence interval's ends are `lower` and `upper`, a range's `start` and `stop`, and a field that is itself a bound `minimum` or `maximum`: `jolty.specs.Yield.lower` and `upper`, for `low` and `high`; the AC, stability and noise reports' `start` and `stop`, for `lowest` and `highest`; and `jolty.results.Excursion.minimum` and `maximum`, for `low` and `high`, with the engine's `jolty_engine::logic::Excursion`, for its `range`, so results files say them, in version 14 of their format.
  `jolty.specs.MIN_PHASE_MARGIN` and `MIN_GAIN_MARGIN`, for `LEAST_PHASE_MARGIN` and `LEAST_GAIN_MARGIN`, and the bipolar transistor's `min_base_resistance`, for `minimum_base_resistance`, beside the MOSFET's `max_drift_velocity`.
  The times a transient steps onto besides its own are its `breakpoints`, ngspice's word, in Python and in the engine: `Transient(..., breakpoints=[...])`, for `times`, so that a result's `times` are every time it solved.
  A temperature coefficient or exponent names in full the parameter it scales: the JFET's and the VDMOS's `transconductance_parameter_temperature_exponent`, for the JFET's `beta_temperature_exponent` and the VDMOS's `transconductance_temperature_exponent`, and their `threshold_voltage_temperature_coefficient`, for `threshold_temperature_coefficient`; the bipolar transistor's `beta_temperature_exponent`, which scales its current gains, keeps its name.
  BSIM3's instance arguments are spelled out as every other device's: `bsim3(threshold_adjustment=..., mobility_multiplier=..., mobility_model=..., capacitance_model=..., noise_model=..., check_parameters=..., charge_partition=...)`, for `delvto`, `mulu0`, `mobmod`, `capmod`, `noimod`, `paramchk` and `xpart`, so SPICE's `DELVTO` has one name on the MOSFET and on BSIM3; its card keeps BSIM3's names, and SPICE cards are read and written under SPICE's.
  A parameter that varies within its tolerance is a `jolty.variation.Toleranced(instance, parameter, nominal, tolerance)`, as the engine's `jolty_engine::variants::Toleranced` is too, for `Varied(path, ...)` and the engine's `path` and `name`.
  A terminal or a net a builder can join is a `jolty.builder.Connectable`, for `Point`, so that a point is only the layout's coordinate.
  A lot's spread of one parameter, its lot's name and the parameter's path, is a `jolty.variation.LotMember`, for `Lot`, so that a lot is only `jolty.ir.Lot`.
  A batch's yield is its report's `yield_`, written `yield` in JSON: `MonteCarloReport.yield_` and `VariantsReport.yield_`, for `passing`, which is a spread's and a yield's count of passing samples.
  The kernels' shapes take their models' full names, as `XShape` for `X`: `VoltageControlledVoltageSourceShape`, `CurrentControlledCurrentSourceShape`, `CoupledInductorsShape`, `LogicDriverShape` and `ThresholdDetectorShape`, for `ControlledVoltageShape`, `SensedCurrentShape`, `CoupledShape`, `DriverShape` and `DetectorShape`.
  A measure resolved for the engine against one kind of result is a `jolty.specs.ResolvedMeasure`, for `Native`, which said how it is computed, and a measure's resolution is its `resolved`, for `native`.
  What results keep of the recorded devices has one vocabulary, in a module `record` for `observe`: `jolty_engine::RecordedValues` at one solution, `RecordedSeries` at every point and `RecordedView` of one point, for `Observation`, `Observations` and `Observed`, and `jolty_engine::Recording`, the index, as Python's `jolty.results.Recording`, for `Recorded`; Python's `jolty.results.RecordedValues`, for `Observed`.
  One run of a batch is a `jolty_engine::batch::Run`, as Python's `jolty.results.Run`, for `Outcome`.
  An unknown, its name and nature, is a `jolty_models::Unknown` in the engine, as `jolty.unknowns.Unknown` in Python, for `UnknownInfo`, and its index in the solution a `jolty_models::UnknownIndex`, for `Unknown`.
  A rule names the fact it states: `low-phase-margin`, `low-gain-margin` and `near-rating`, which pairs with `over-rating`, for `phase-margin`, `gain-margin` and `derating`, so a configuration that names the old ones is refused as any unknown rule is; their thresholds `jolty.checks.MIN_PHASE_MARGIN`, `MIN_GAIN_MARGIN` and `DERATING_FACTOR` stand beside the rules, for `jolty.specs.MIN_PHASE_MARGIN`, `MIN_GAIN_MARGIN` and `jolty.checks.DERATED`.
  A SHA-256 that identifies what a file or a shard holds is its digest: `jolty.files.circuit_digest` and `plan_digest`, for `circuit_hash` and `plan_hash`; `jolty.shards.digest` and every shard's `digest`, for `fingerprint`, in shard files too, in version 3 of their format; and `jolty.spice.fetch(url, digest)`, for `sha256`.
  A signal's arithmetic mean over its window is `jolty.specs.mean`, as a statistic's `mean` is, and the engine's `Measure::Mean`, for `average` and `Measure::Average`.
  The diagnostics a check found are a `jolty.checks.CheckReport`, for `Report`, so that `Report` alone is never a class.
  A characterized logic model's summary in a report is a `jolty.fidelity.CharacterizedModel`, for `Model`, so that a model is a device's.
  A noise source's contribution in a report is a `jolty.report.NoiseContribution`, with its source's `name`, in a `NoiseReport`'s `contributions`, as a result's, for `NoiseContributor`, `noise` and `contributors`.
  The vectors one analysis of ngspice left are a `jolty.spice.ngspice.Result`, as `jolty.spice.xyce.Result` is, for `Raw`.
  The engine's types say what they are: `jolty_models::Differentiable`, a model whose instances give exact derivatives, for `Native`; `jolty_models::mosfet::ModelLevel`, for `Level`; `jolty_engine::logic::ExcursionKind`, what left a logic device's domain, for `Bound`; and `jolty_numerics::integration::ErrorBound`, what bounds a step's error, for `Bound`.
  The temperature another simulator must be given so its thermal voltage is jolty's is `jolty.spice.ngspice.option_temperature` and `jolty.spice.xyce.option_temperature`, and a dialect's `option_temperature`, for `spice_celsius`, `xyce_celsius` and `celsius`, so that `celsius` is only `jolty.units.celsius`.
  A deck given as its lines is a `deck` in every function that takes one: `jolty.spice.ngspice.run(deck, ...)`, `runs(deck, ...)` and `jolty.spice.xyce.run(deck, ...)`, for `lines`, as `execute(deck)`.
  The report's limits have the shape of `max_diagnostics`: the settings `max_unknowns` and `max_output`, and `JOLTY_MAX_UNKNOWNS` and `JOLTY_MAX_OUTPUT`, for `listed_unknowns`, `output_limit`, `JOLTY_LISTED_UNKNOWNS` and `JOLTY_OUTPUT_LIMIT`, read from `pyproject.toml` and the environment; `jolty.report.report` takes the first as `max_unknowns`, for `listed`.
  Each MCP tool is its command's verb and object: `view_circuit`, `cost_circuit` and `explain_rules` for `write_viewer`, `price_circuit` and `explain_rule`; and the KiCad command is a verb in every layer, `jolty export-kicad`, `jolty.service.export_kicad`, `ExportKicadRequest`, `ExportKicadResult` and the MCP tool `export_kicad_netlist`, for `jolty kicad`, `write_kicad`, `KicadRequest`, `KicadResult` and `write_kicad_netlist`.
- Every results file states its results' provenance, in version 9 of the format: the jolty, the build and the platform that simulated them, and the hashes of the circuit and the plan they are of, which `jolty simulate` gives; `jolty.files.load_provenance` reads it, and `load_results` given a circuit refuses a file whose results are of another.
  `build`, `platform` and `circuit_hash` move from `jolty.shards` to `jolty.files`, beside the new `plan_hash`, and a shard's fingerprint reads them there.
- Each result states where its analysis used logic devices outside their domains, its `excursions`, as `Simulation.run` watched it, and results files keep them, in version 8 of their format; `Simulation` has no `excursions` attribute, which held the last analysis's, so that what a result said depended on the order of the calls.
  A result that no run watched, a run of a batch, a run in ngspice or a result within another's, states `None`, and an analysis that fails states none, where the simulation held what it found before failing.
  A results file's header states its format's version, which it gave as 6 since version 7.
- A decade sweep's frequencies, `jolty.plan.decade`, are computed in decimal arithmetic and rounded once, each the float nearest its exact value, so that every platform finds the same: each platform's `log10` and `pow` rounded them differently, so one plan swept other frequencies on Linux than on macOS, and most of them move by a few units in their last place.
- A Gear2 step's truncation error is estimated with Gear2's error constant at the step's ratio `ω` to the one before it, `(1 + ω)²/(6ω(1 + 2ω))`, and the longest step after it, or in its place where its error rejects it, is solved for at that step's own ratio, where SPICE takes the constant step's 2/9 at every step, which after a step cut to a tenth estimates the error 7.6 times too small.
  Gear2 runs take other steps, each held to its tolerance after the step's length changes; the trapezoidal formula, the default, and backward Euler, whose constants do not depend on the step before, are unchanged.
- `charge_tolerance` means SPICE's `CHGTOL`: a time step holds a charge `q` to `relative_tolerance · max(|q|, charge_tolerance)`, as ngspice 47's `cktterr.c` does at lines 40 and 41, where it held it to `relative_tolerance · |q| + charge_tolerance`, which let the charge of any net holding less than about 10 pC err by the 10 fC the option added, whatever the relative tolerance: a thousandfold tighter `relative_tolerance` changed no step of a low-pass of 1 fF or 10 fF.
  Transients of small nets take more steps: at default options the benchmarks' chain of 500 inverters accepts 2,448 steps where it accepted 749, in 2.2 to 2.3 times the time, and their buck converter 82,710 where it accepted 73,880, in 1.11 to 1.12 times; the examples, whose longest steps bound theirs, change by less than 1%.
  The differential tests run both engines at the charge tolerance's default, 1e-14 C, which both now read alike, where they ran at 1e-18 C.
- A transient's shortest step is SPICE's, 10⁻¹¹ of its longest (ngspice 47's `traninit.c`, line 36), where it was 10⁻⁹: a charge behind a small resistance settles within picoseconds of a corner, and resolving it to SPICE's charge tolerance can take steps shorter than 10⁻⁹ of the longest, as the benchmarks' buck converter's switches' internal sources do at a relative tolerance of 1e-5, which stopped it at 0.39 ms.
- A time step's Newton iteration goes on past `transient_iterations` as long as each step is shorter than the one before, and is accepted once its last step, times `θ/(1 − θ)` where it contracts by more than half from one step to the next, is within tolerance: an iteration that converges linearly, as Newton's does at a double root, such as a node only gmin holds beside a transistor at its threshold, finishes where it stopped at the count and shortened the step until none was left.
  At default options and the former charge tolerance, the benchmarks' buck converter rejects 10,821 steps where it rejected 16,894 and takes 245,866 Newton iterations where it took 299,616; with SPICE's, their chain of 500 inverters accepts 2,448 steps where it would accept 2,705, and its edges lie 0.23 ns on average from a reference at a relative tolerance of 1e-6 where they would lie 0.085 ns, as the steps an iteration stopped at the count shortened were shorter than their error asked.
- A yield's Wilson interval is exact at its ends: its lower end is 0 when no sample passes, and its upper end 1 when every sample does, where they were rounding, such as 1.084e-17 for none of 2,000.
- jolty requires Python 3.15 or newer, the version CI tests and the one whose lazy modules (PEP 810) start a command without importing matplotlib; the extension is built for the stable ABI of 3.15.
- Monte Carlo samples are drawn by the engine, each factor from the seed, the sample's index and the name of its part or its lot alone, through ChaCha20, so that sample `i` is the same drawn alone or among any others, on any thread, and a part added leaves every other part's draws as they were.
  A seed therefore draws other samples than it did before, `jolty.variation.sample` takes the seed and the sample's index in place of a numpy generator, and a seed lies from 0 to 2⁶⁴ − 1.
- Monte Carlo analyses, corners and worst cases run as batches on one circuit elaborated per structure, the corners that change only the temperature taking it as their runs' values, a Monte Carlo analysis's samples as a batch at each condition, rather than one simulation at a time on a thread per core.
  Their reports are the same, bit for bit, but for the Monte Carlo spreads' last digits: a sample now runs at exactly the numbers the engine draws, where a sample sent to the engine as JSON text ran at the number its parser read back, which differs by an ulp for about one number in five.
- The Python extension allocates through mimalloc, whose heaps are each thread's own, so that a batch's runs on many threads take no longer each than on one: 95.9% of linear on eight threads, against 64 to 68% with the system's allocator.
- The engine reads every number exactly as Python writes it, a circuit's values, an analysis's times and frequencies and the options alike, through serde_json's `float_roundtrip`: before, about one number in five was read an ulp off, as 981.4902141434463 read as 981.4902141434465, so results change in their last digits.
- A measurement may read an infinity, as the gain of a response of exactly nothing is −∞ dB, and its verdict judges it, with infinite slack; only a reading that is not a number is unmeasured.
  A twin T at its notch passes exactly nothing once its numbers are read exactly, where it was unmeasured.
  JSON, which has no infinity, writes one as `"Infinity"` or `"-Infinity"`, as the reports' schemas say, and a spread's mean and deviation are null where its values leave them undefined.
