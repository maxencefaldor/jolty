# Departures from ngspice

jolty agrees with ngspice where ngspice is accurate and diverges where another formulation is more accurate, more robust, or intentionally configurable (such as per-step Newton iteration limits).
The tables below catalog every known difference between jolty and ngspice 47, each with source-line or benchmark evidence and a link to the corresponding guide section.
Source references cite the default ngspice 47 release built with XSPICE.
Benchmark measurements on the synchronous buck converter (`benchmarks/speed/circuits/buck.py`) cover its 2 ms transient at default truncation tolerance on an Apple M2 Max (14 and 15 November 2026).

Differential tests compare every quantity except these documented departures under matched tightened tolerances, with jolty stepping onto every ngspice time point ([Simulation](simulation.md#how-the-engine-solves-a-circuit)).

## Time steps and their error

Step selection and local truncation error estimation are detailed in [the table of methods](simulation.md#how-the-engine-solves-a-circuit).

| Departure | ngspice 47 | jolty | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Charges judged | Level 1, 2, and 3 MOSFETs check only gate charges and ignore bulk junctions (`mos1trun.c`, lines 24 to 26; `mos2trun.c`, `mos3trun.c`). BSIM3 checks bulk, gate, and drain charges (`b3trunc.c`, lines 38 to 40) with junctions folded in (`b3ld.c`, lines 2797 to 2802) | Checks the charge at every node (including MOSFET bulk junctions) and every Meyer displacement (carrying the full oxide charge across each capacitance) | On the buck benchmark, ngspice takes 56,624 points vs jolty's 103,349 tried steps (143,339 checking junctions). On the 500-inverter chain, jolty takes 2,448 steps (0.23 ns mean error vs `1e-6` ref); ngspice takes 903 points (0.51 ns) and needs 3,602 at `1e-4` (0.22 ns) |
| Error filter | Uses raw divided differences directly (`cktterr.c`, lines 43 to 59) | Filters divided differences through the step equations, `C (G + αC)⁻¹ α` | Unfiltered estimates miscount fast components damped within a single step as truncation error (Shampine, 1980; Hairer and Wanner, 1996, IV.8) |
| Gear2 error constant | Uses constant-step 2/9 regardless of step-size ratio (`cktterr.c`, lines 24 to 31) | Uses variable-step `(1 + ω)²/(6ω(1 + 2ω))` for step ratio `ω` | In exact arithmetic on a cubic, true error is 7.56× the 2/9 estimate after a 10× step cut and 1.69× after halving; unit tests cover ratios from 0.1 to 10 |
| First step after a corner | Evaluates order-1 divided differences reaching back across the corner without restarting history, then immediately tries order 2 (`dctran.c`, lines 489 to 494, 821 to 833; `cktterr.c`, lines 44 to 59) | Restarts integration history at the corner and estimates first-step error from shared curvature with the second step | Points preceding a corner have different curvature from steps after the corner |
| Step acceptance rule | Accepts when the error-permitted step exceeds 0.9× the tried step (`dctran.c`, line 801) | Accepts only when error meets tolerance, then scales the next step to 0.9× the permitted step | On the buck benchmark, SPICE's rule took 73,299 accepted and 12,527 rejected steps compared with jolty's 65,606 accepted and 7,807 rejected steps |
| Tolerance scaling with step | Evaluates every tolerance at the tried step length, even though current tolerance scales with step length (`cktterr.c`, lines 37 to 42, 69 to 76) | Solves for the largest step meeting each tolerance as it scales: fixed charge tolerance and step-proportional current tolerance | Integrated current tolerance equals a current bound multiplied by the step duration |
| Divided-difference floor | Floors error constant times divided difference at `abstol` (`cktterr.c`, line 69) | Applies no floor; a vanishing divided difference permits any step up to the maximum step limit | `abstol` is in amperes while a charge divided difference has units `C / s^(p+1)`, making a floor dependent on time units |
| Flux absolute tolerance | Uses `abstol + reltol · |V|` (1e-12 V by default) for inductor flux (`cktterr.c`, lines 37 to 38; `indtrunc.c`, line 24) | Uses `voltage_tolerance + relative_tolerance · |V|` because flux equations constrain voltages | Adding a current tolerance (`abstol`) directly to a voltage mixes incompatible dimensions |
| Two steps before a breakpoint | Equalizes step sizes whenever 1.9 steps would cross a breakpoint (`dctran.c`, lines 588 to 598) | Steps directly onto the breakpoint without pre-equalizing | On the buck benchmark, pre-equalization increased accepted steps by 11% and rejected steps by 47% |
| Default maximum step | Uses print step `TSTEP` when under 1/50 of run duration unless `nostepsizelimit` is set (`traninit.c`, lines 26 to 34) | Defaults to 1/50 of run duration because jolty transients have no print step | Cross-simulator comparisons set matching explicit maximum steps in both engines |
| Minimum time step | Uses a fixed `10⁻¹¹ · max_step` floor (`traninit.c`, line 36) | Derives the floor from IEEE-754 binary64 resolution as `16 ε · max(t, scale)` with `scale = min(max_step, first_breakpoint)` | Retains at least 48 bits of precision in divided differences while guaranteeing `t + h > t` (Shampine and Reichelt, 1997, §2; Hairer, Nørsett, and Wanner, 1993, §II.4); lets wide-time-scale stiff circuits (such as a 1 ns step over 40 s in `crates/jolty-engine/tests/analytic.rs`) resolve sub-picosecond initial steps where `10⁻¹¹ · max_step` fails |
| Numerical ringing | Applies trapezoidal integration throughout; `xmu < 0.5` damps every step uniformly (`cktntask.c`, lines 113 to 119) | Inserts a backward Euler step only when alternating derivatives confirm numerical ringing via a restoring-force check, linearized passivity `uᵀ S G u ≥ uᵀ S (αC) u` (`F_{i,i} < 0.5` for Meyer displacements), and saddle-point projection for capacitive loops | Trapezoidal leaves stiff real modes (`σ > 2/h`) and algebraic `R = -1` constraints undamped, while lossless LC modes (`ωh > 2`) alternate legitimately. Separating them preserves LC tank energy over 20 periods at `reltol = 1e-2` while damping corners and zero-capacitance Meyer displacements |
| Behavioral source jumps | Steps across discontinuities wherever they fall | Locates jumps within the step, lands on each discontinuity, and restarts integration | ngspice can stall on unlocated behavioral jumps ([Simulation](simulation.md#how-the-engine-solves-a-circuit)) |
| Step prediction | Each device extrapolates internal junction voltages independently (`mos1load.c`, lines 200 to 222) | Extrapolates global node voltages directly | Equivalent in practice: first iterates agree at traced switching edges on the buck benchmark |
| Retry after Newton iteration limit | Cuts the step by 8 and re-predicts the next attempt (`dctran.c`, line 733) | Cuts the step by 8 and continues Newton iteration from the previous iterate | Slowly converging iterations (such as a MOSFET turning off with a floating source) keep progress from the prior iterate |

Current `i` carried by a charge is bounded, as in SPICE, by `abstol + reltol · |i|` over the step, scaled by `trtol` (`cktterr.c`, lines 37 to 42).
SPICE omits the factorial converting a divided difference into a derivative, while jolty includes it so `truncation_tolerance` bounds error with the same semantics as SPICE `trtol`.

## Newton iteration

Newton convergence behavior is detailed in [the table of methods](simulation.md#how-the-engine-solves-a-circuit).

| Departure | ngspice 47 | jolty | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Iterations per time step | Enforces at least 100 iterations regardless of `ITL4` (default 10) or DC sweep `ITL2` (50) (`niiter.c`, lines 37 to 39; `dctran.c`, line 709; `cktntask.c`, line 106) | Enforces `max_transient_iterations` (default 10, matching documented `ITL4`) and judges the post-limit iterate; DC sweeps use `max_dc_iterations` (100) | Raising the per-step limit to 100 cuts buck benchmark rejected steps by 24% and total Newton iterations by 14% |
| Convergence test | Checks unknown updates against `reltol` and `vntol`/`abstol` (`niconv.c`, lines 40 to 80) plus per-device current predictions (`niconv.c`, lines 82 to 95; `NEWCONV` in `macros.h`, line 19), without checking residuals | Checks updates (scaled by `θ/(1 - θ)` for contracting transient steps), requires every residual within tolerance plus floating-point rounding floor, and rejects limited iterates | Residual checks enforce Kirchhoff's laws, rounding floors let milliohm branches at high voltages converge, and `θ/(1 - θ)` bounds remaining error once contraction `θ > 0.5` (Deuflhard, 2004, section 2.1) |
| Behavioral source convergence | Checks current value against previous value within `reltol` and `vntol`/`abstol` without a rounding floor on input unknowns (`asrcconv.c`, lines 41 to 55) | Checks equation residual with the floating-point rounding floor | ngspice 47 timed out after 300 s on the [memristive NOR](../discovery/memristive-nor.md) `high`-corner transient under both jolty and ngspice tolerances |
| Limiting and convergence | Only the junction limiter blocks convergence; `fetlim` and `limvds` do not (`mos1load.c`, lines 376 to 384, 719 to 720; `b3ld.c`, lines 187, 359 to 366, 2437 to 2438) | All three voltage limiters prevent premature convergence | A limited iterate does not satisfy the circuit equations |
| First-iteration limiting reference | Resets MOSFET state to the last accepted point before predicting (`mos1load.c`, lines 201 to 208) | Limits relative to the device's most recent evaluation (the previous iterate after a rejected step) | Limiting from the last accepted point saves 1.5% of steps on the buck benchmark |
| Junction limiter | Uses ngspice's custom `pnjlim`, which steps backward toward `-∞` just above `2vt` (`devsup.c`, lines 54 to 61) | Uses SPICE3 `pnjlim`, stepping at most `vt · ln(1 + Δ/vt)` beyond `2vt` | ngspice's limiter halves failed Newton iterations on the buck benchmark (2,768 to 1,289) without changing step count; both jump at `2vt` |

## Operating points and sweeps

Operating-point continuation and multi-solution search are described in [the table of methods](simulation.md#how-the-engine-solves-a-circuit) and [Several DC solutions](simulation.md#several-dc-solutions).

| Departure | ngspice 47 | jolty | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Newton starting point | Initializes junctions at critical voltage and MOSFETs at `vbs = -1`, `vgs = VTO`, `vds = 0` (`dioload.c`, lines 162 to 165; `mos1load.c`, lines 397 to 408) | Initializes all unknowns to zero | Not benchmarked separately |
| Convergence continuation | Runs dynamic gmin stepping, true gmin stepping (`cktop.c`, lines 57 to 77), Gillespie source stepping (lines 85 to 92), then an unchecked 10 µs transient at 100 ns steps (`init.c`, lines 77 to 93) | Steps node-to-ground conductance, junction gmin, and source scale, then runs pseudo-transient continuation until steady state | Pseudo-transient continuation traverses folds where source stepping stalls at 51% and matches ngspice's transient operating point to printed digits |
| Failed DC sweep point | Re-solves a full operating point from default initialization (`dctrcurv.c`, lines 306 to 313), which can jump branches | Approaches the target value from the last converged point via step halving | Step halving keeps the sweep on the active solution branch |
| Multiple DC solutions | Does not search beyond the first solution | Discovers additional valid DC solutions via deflation | [Several DC solutions](simulation.md#several-dc-solutions) |
| Open-circuit transfer resistance | Reports `10²⁰` Ω when no current flows (`tfanal.c`, lines 125 to 126) | Reports infinite resistance | Zero current implies infinite impedance ([Analyses](simulation.md#analyses)) |

## Linear solves

| Departure | ngspice 47 | jolty | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pivot threshold | Uses `0.001` times the column maximum (`cktntask.c`, line 124) | Uses `0.1` times the column maximum | On random circuit matrices, backward errors stay below `10⁻¹⁴` in jolty vs up to `10⁻⁸` at `0.001` ([the table of methods](simulation.md#how-the-engine-solves-a-circuit)) |
| Resistance below 1 Ω | Stamps as a plain conductance | Introduces a dedicated branch-current unknown | Conductances above 1 S leave fewer than four significant digits for picosiemens shunts; results differ only by rounding |

## Small-signal analyses

Small-signal analyses linearize the circuit at the operating point for AC, noise, stability, sensitivity, and pole-zero sweeps.

| Departure | ngspice 47 | jolty | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Linear AC sensitivity frequency step | Multiplies each frequency by the step after the first because `inc_freq` checks AC `LINEAR` (3) instead of `SENS_LINEAR` (15) (`cktsens.c`, line 831; `acdefs.h`, line 40; `sensdefs.h`, lines 80 to 87) | Evaluates at the exact requested linear frequencies | Confirmed on the first coverage-matrix run where only the initial frequency agreed |
| Iterative refinement on `G + jωC` | Solves once using pivots reused from the previous frequency | Refines whenever componentwise backward error exceeds machine epsilon and shrinks by half per step | A series RLC far above resonance achieves 15.6 correct digits in jolty vs 11.4 digits in ngspice KLU |

## Devices

Each device section in the guide also documents model-specific departures.

| Departure | ngspice 47 | jolty | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Physical constants | Uses CODATA 2014 constants: `k = 1.38064852e-23` J/K and `q = 1.6021766208e-19` C (`const.h`, lines 32 and 37) | Uses exact SI constants: `k = 1.380649e-23` J/K and `q = 1.602176634e-19` C | Differential tests offset ngspice temperature to match thermal voltage ([Devices](devices.md)) |
| Analytical derivatives | Omits or miscomputes derivatives in BJT base resistance, forward-bulk MOSFET threshold, BSIM3 bulk clamp and `capmod` 0 poly depletion, BSIM4 `capmod` 2 body capacitances, and SKY130 RF PMOS `gds`. VDMOS quasi-saturation, subthreshold, reverse CLM, and negative-base behavioral `POW` also err (`inpptree.c`, lines 613 to 632) | Computes exact derivatives via automatic differentiation | Documented under [bipolar transistor](devices.md#bipolar-transistor), [MOSFET](devices.md#mosfet), [BSIM3](devices.md#bsim3), [BSIM4](devices.md#bsim4), and [power MOSFET](devices.md#power-mosfet) |
| Meyer transient capacitances | Accumulates `q₁ + C̄ · Δv` and integrates using only derivative `C̄` (`mos1load.c`, lines 821 to 827, 863 to 868) | Formulates `C(v) · dv/dt` via displacement unknowns with exact derivatives | Neither formulation conserves charge because no scalar charge potential yields Meyer capacitances ([MOSFET](devices.md#mosfet)) |
| VDMOS gate-drain charge | Integrates capacitance with the trapezoidal rule between consecutive steps | Integrates capacitance analytically as a conserved charge function | [Power MOSFET](devices.md#power-mosfet) |
| BJT excess phase in AC | Applies a pure time delay in AC | Evaluates the same Bessel filter integrated in transient analysis | Differs by 5% in gain at `PTF = 30°` ([Bipolar transistor](devices.md#bipolar-transistor)) |
| VBIC PNP excess phase | Breaks NPN/PNP mirror symmetry: 3.01 mA DC collector current vs 56.7 µA mirror value (`vbicload.c`, lines 449, 485, 486, 1139) | Mirrors NPN exactly | [VBIC](devices.md#vbic) |
| VBIC parasitic base resistance noise | Stamps thermal noise across the emitter resistance (`vbicnoise.c`, lines 122 to 123) | Stamps thermal noise across the parasitic base resistance | [VBIC](devices.md#vbic) |

| BSIM4 gate electrode resistance | Computes gate resistance from the last prepared bin's drawn length and clamps non-1/2 `ngcon` only after first preparation (`b4v5temp.c`, lines 382, 1562, 1825; `b4v5check.c`, line 262) | Computes gate resistance from each instance's length and specified `ngcon` | Verified directly in ngspice source ([BSIM4](devices.md#bsim4)) |
| Switches in AC | ngspice 42 XSPICE switches negate small-signal conductance sign relative to DC | Exports switches to ngspice as behavioral current sources matching the DC law | [Switches](devices.md#switches) |
| Self-heating | Restricted to dedicated electrothermal models such as VBIC | Supported uniformly on every semiconductor connected to a thermal net | [Self-heating](devices.md#self-heating) |
| Non-integer power of a negative base | Evaluates to 0 in behavioral sources and NaN in parameters (`ptfuncs.c`, lines 77 to 86) | Raises the absolute value of the base to the exponent | Direct mathematical definition |
| Double-quoted card parameter outside braces | Drops the quoted value with a warning and falls back to the default | Evaluates the expression identically to braced values | On SKY130 `res_generic_po`, ngspice drops the narrowing expression, shifting resistance by 0.56% |
| Element multiplier inside multiplied subcircuit | Overrides the element's `m` with the subcircuit multiplier outside HSPICE mode (`inpcom.c`, lines 4257 to 4283) | Multiplies subcircuit and element multipliers in every mode | Matches HSPICE compatibility behavior in ngspice |
